⚠️ This is a general guide only. Your own recovery may be faster or slower depending on your fracture, the surgery done, and how your body heals. Always follow what Dr. Satish Reddy or your physiotherapist tells you at your visits, even if it's different from this page.
Phase 1

Protect the Wrist

Week 0 – Week 2

🎯 Goals

  • Bring down the swelling in your hand and fingers
  • Keep the splint clean and dry
  • Keep your fingers, elbow, and shoulder moving so they don't get stiff
  • Manage pain comfortably

⚠️ What to Avoid

  • Keep the splint on at all times, day and night
  • Do not lift, push, pull, or grip anything with the operated hand
  • Do not remove the splint yourself to "check" the wound
  • Do not let the hand hang down for long periods

Exercises (Start From Day 1)

  • Finger bending: make a soft fist and open fully, several times a day
  • Finger spreading: spread fingers apart, then bring them together
  • Thumb movement: move your thumb up, down, and across your palm
  • Elbow movement: bend and straighten your elbow fully
  • Shoulder movement: move your shoulder in all directions, including rolling your shoulders back
  • Shoulder circles: gentle circles forward and backward
  • Neck movement: gentle turns and tilts if your neck feels tight from holding your arm carefully
  • Deep breathing: slow, deep breaths a few times through the day
  • Keep the hand elevated (raised) when sitting or lying down

🧊 Swelling Control

  • Keep your hand raised above the level of your heart as much as possible, especially when resting
  • An ice pack (wrapped in cloth, never placed directly on skin) over the splint can help in the first few days
  • Keep moving your fingers often. This "pump" action reduces swelling
  • Avoid tight rings, bangles, or watches on the operated hand until swelling settles

✅ Ready for Phase 2 When:

  • Wound has been checked and stitches removed at your 2-week visit
  • Swelling is coming down
  • Fingers are moving well and are not stiff
  • Pain is controlled with simple painkillers
Phase 2

Start Moving the Wrist

Week 2 – Week 6

🎯 Goals

  • Get full finger movement back
  • Start gentle wrist and forearm movement
  • Begin simple hand activities, like picking up small objects
  • Take care of the healing scar

⚠️ What to Avoid

  • Still no lifting, pushing, pulling, or gripping with the operated hand
  • No strengthening exercises yet
  • Do not force the wrist to bend or straighten with pressure from the other hand

Exercises

  • Continue finger bending and straightening exercises
  • If a finger is not straightening fully, gently help it straighten with your other hand and hold for a few seconds
  • Palm turning: turn your palm face up, then face down
  • Wrist movement: gently move your wrist up, down, and side to side within comfortable range
  • From Week 4 onward, you may gently and slowly assist the wrist movement a little further with your other hand

🩹 Scar Care

  • Once the wound has fully healed and stitches are out, gently massage the scar with a plain moisturising cream
  • Massage in small circles for a few minutes, 2–3 times a day
  • This softens the scar and reduces tightness around it

🏠 Daily Life Adjustments

  • Eating and writing: use the other hand for now if the operated hand tires quickly
  • Cooking, washing clothes, mopping: avoid until strength returns; ask family for help
  • Auto/car travel: short journeys are fine; avoid gripping handles tightly with the operated hand
  • Phone use: light use is fine; avoid holding a heavy phone for long in the operated hand

✅ Ready for Phase 3 When:

  • Fingers move fully and easily
  • Wrist moves gently in all directions without sharp pain
  • Wound and scar have healed well
  • You can do light one-handed tasks with the other hand comfortably while the operated hand rests
Phase 3

Build Strength

Week 6 – Week 8

🎯 Goals

  • Begin gentle strengthening of the wrist and forearm
  • Regain full wrist and forearm movement
  • Improve hand coordination and grip control
  • Start using the hand for light daily tasks

⚠️ What to Avoid

  • Do not put weight through the wrist, such as pushing yourself up from a chair with that hand, until your doctor clears you (usually around Week 10–12)
  • Do not force the wrist into a painful stretch
  • Do not use the wrong wrist muscles to "cheat" a movement; ask your physiotherapist to check your technique

Exercises

  • Gentle, sustained wrist and forearm stretches to work on any remaining stiffness
  • Wrist strengthening exercises as shown by your physiotherapist
  • Gentle squeezing exercises using a soft ball or therapy putty
  • Gradually increasing resistance exercises for wrist and forearm, as advised
  • Light shoulder and elbow strengthening from Week 8

🏠 Daily Life Adjustments

  • Light cooking, dusting, folding clothes: okay from Week 6–7 in small amounts
  • Temple visits, walking outdoors: fine; avoid carrying bags in the operated hand
  • Two-wheeler riding: avoid until Week 10–12; gripping the handle and clutch needs full strength
  • Driving a car: only once you can grip the steering wheel firmly and comfortably, usually from Week 8 onward

✅ Ready for Phase 4 When:

  • Wrist and forearm move almost fully in all directions
  • Grip is improving and daily light tasks feel comfortable
  • No significant pain with the exercises above
  • Cleared by your doctor for weight-bearing through the wrist
Phase 4

Back to Normal Activities

Week 8 – Week 12

🎯 Goals

  • Gradually return to your normal daily activities
  • Regain full grip strength
  • Return to work, hobbies, and most sports, except heavy lifting

Exercises

  • Continue and progress all Phase 3 exercises
  • Gradually increase grip strengthening (hand gripper, stress ball)
  • Weight-bearing exercises through the hand, such as gentle push-up preparation against a wall, as cleared by your doctor
  • Return to sport-specific or work-specific hand movements gradually

🚨 When to Call Dr. Satish Reddy / Come to Hospital Immediately

🔴 Emergency Signs

  • Fingers become cold, pale, blue, or completely numb
  • Severe pain that is not relieved by your painkillers
  • The splint feels very tight and swelling is increasing rapidly
  • High fever with redness, warmth, or pus from the wound
  • A fall or injury to the operated arm

🟡 Call the Clinic Within 24 Hours If:

  • Swelling suddenly increases without an obvious reason
  • Tingling or pins-and-needles in the fingers that does not settle
  • The wound looks red, is oozing, or has an odour
  • You are unable to move your fingers as well as before
  • Pain is getting worse instead of better as the days pass
📅 Follow-up Schedule

🗓 Routine Follow-ups

  • 2 weeks: Wound check, stitch removal
  • 6 weeks: Movement and healing check, X-ray if needed
  • 3 months: Strength and function review

🏋 Long-term Advice

  • Continue wrist and grip exercises for a few months even after things feel normal
  • Build grip strength gradually with a soft hand gripper
  • Wear a wrist guard for high-risk sports or activities for the first year if advised

🏠 Daily Life Tips

  • Ask family for help with heavy kitchen or cleaning work in the first 6 weeks
  • Keep the hand raised when resting, especially in the first 2 weeks
  • Wear loose sleeves that don't press on the splint or the healing wrist
