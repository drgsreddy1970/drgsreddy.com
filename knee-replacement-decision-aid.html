Knee Osteoarthritis · Decision Aid

Understanding your knee options

A short, guided walk through the choices for a painful arthritic knee, from weight loss and physiotherapy to injections and knee replacement, so you can talk to your surgeon with clarity.

Before you begin

This is a tool to prepare you, not to diagnose you

It takes about five minutes. Your answers stay on your own phone or computer and are not sent anywhere or saved by us.

What this tool does: it helps you understand what usually happens with an arthritic knee, lays out every option side by side, and gives you a one page summary with the right questions to ask.

What it does not do: it cannot examine you, read your X-ray, or tell you which treatment is right for you. Only a consultation and examination can do that. If your knee needs urgent attention, the next step will help you recognise that.
Step 1 of 5 · Safety check

First, a quick safety check

Some knee problems need attention sooner rather than later. Please answer honestly. This screen can only tell you to seek care sooner, never that everything is fine.

Is your knee hot, badly swollen, and very painful, together with fever or feeling unwell?Fever meaning a temperature above 100.4°F, or shivering and chills.
Did your knee pain begin right after a fall or injury, and you now cannot put weight on that leg at all?
Is your knee stuck so that you genuinely cannot straighten or bend it, as if it is jammed?A truly locked knee, not simply a stiff or aching one.
Is your calf (below the knee) painful, swollen, warm, or red on that same leg?Especially if this came on over a day or two without an injury.
Please answer all four questions to continue.

Step 5 of 5 · Your options

Your options, side by side

For an arthritic knee these are the usual paths. They are not either or. Most people move through the earlier ones first, and the right time for surgery is a shared decision with your surgeon.

1

Weight and activity

Every kilogram lost takes several times that load off the knee with each step. Along with switching to knee friendly movement such as walking, cycling, or swimming, this is the foundation the other options build on.

Suits

  • Everyone, at any stage
  • Those carrying extra weight
  • Early to moderate arthritis

Keep in mind

  • Takes months, not days
  • Needs steady effort
  • Helps pain but does not reverse the arthritis
2

Physiotherapy and exercise

Strengthening the muscles around the knee, especially the thigh, can meaningfully reduce pain and improve how the joint feels and functions. A guided programme works far better than exercising blindly.

Suits

  • Most people with knee arthritis
  • Those wanting to delay or avoid surgery
  • Preparing for surgery, if chosen later

Keep in mind

  • Needs regular practice
  • Benefit builds over weeks
  • Does not repair the joint surface
3

Pain relief medicines

Tablets, gels, and creams can ease flares and keep you moving. They are best used thoughtfully and for defined periods rather than continuously, and some need care if you have kidney, stomach, or heart conditions.

Suits

  • Flare ups and bad patches
  • Alongside exercise and weight loss

Keep in mind

  • Manages pain, not the cause
  • Long term use needs supervision
  • Not for everyone
4

Injections into the knee

An injection can quieten a painful knee for a period of weeks to months. It can be a useful way to get through a difficult phase, or to buy time, though the relief is temporary and it does not change the underlying arthritis.

Suits

  • A painful flare that is not settling
  • Buying time before deciding on surgery

Keep in mind

  • Relief is temporary
  • Cannot be repeated endlessly
  • Does not halt the arthritis
5

Knee replacement surgery

When arthritis is advanced and the earlier steps are no longer keeping pace with the pain, replacing the worn joint surfaces gives reliable, lasting relief for most people. It is a considered step, not a last resort, and the right moment is one you choose together with your surgeon.

Modern knee replacements last many years for the great majority of people. Recovery involves several weeks of rehabilitation, and the effort you put into that rehabilitation strongly shapes the result.

Suits

  • Advanced arthritis on X-ray
  • Pain that is ruining sleep and daily life
  • When earlier steps no longer help enough

Keep in mind

  • A recovery of several weeks
  • Small risks, as with any surgery
  • Best chosen at the right time, not too early
