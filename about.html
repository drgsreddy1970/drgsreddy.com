The story

A surgeon shaped by three continents

Dr. Satish Reddy Gandavarapu did not plan to build a subspecialty. He trained at the best institutions he could find, brought that training back to Hyderabad, and set about offering his patients the kind of surgical care that had previously required going abroad. What followed was the founding of orthopaedic oncology as a clinical discipline in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, a region where bone tumour patients had previously had few options.

After graduating as Gold Medallist in MS Orthopaedics, having received the Nicholas Andre Medal, the highest academic distinction in his cohort. He pursued fellowships at three of the world's most respected orthopaedic institutions: the Rizzoli Orthopaedic Institute in Bologna, the Royal Orthopaedic Hospital in Birmingham, and Flinders Medical Centre in Adelaide. Each was chosen for a specific subspecialty that did not yet exist in his home region.

On returning to Hyderabad, he joined the faculty at Osmania Medical College and Hospital as Assistant Professor before going on to establish Prime Hospitals, which later became Aster Prime Hospital, serving as its Executive Director and Managing Director through the hospital's growth from a single unit into a significant private healthcare provider that attracted private equity investment. He exited in 2022 by selling his stake to the Aster Group.

Today he consults at KIMS Hospital, Ministers Road, Secunderabad, where he continues to practice across orthopaedic oncology, joint replacement, sports surgery, complex trauma, and upper limb surgery.

"I went to Bologna and Birmingham and Adelaide not for the CV line, but because the patients I would eventually see in Hyderabad deserved a surgeon who had trained where the best work was being done."

Entrepreneurship

Building a hospital from scratch

Alongside his clinical career, Dr. Satish founded Prime Hospitals in Hyderabad, an orthopaedic-led private hospital built from the ground up. He served as its Executive Director and Managing Director, overseeing clinical governance, hospital operations, and strategic growth.

Under his leadership, Prime Hospitals grew into Aster Prime Hospital, attracting private equity investment and expanding its clinical footprint significantly. In 2022, Dr. Satish exited the business by selling his stake to the Aster Group, one of India's largest healthcare conglomerates.

This experience, building, scaling, and managing a hospital, gives Dr. Satish a rare perspective that few purely clinical surgeons possess: a direct understanding of hospital governance, quality systems, insurance processes, and what patients experience beyond the operating theatre.

He subsequently completed the Advanced Management Programme in Healthcare at the Indian School of Business, Hyderabad, graduating as Valedictorian of his cohort.

Beyond the operating theatre

The surgeon who runs marathons

Dr. Satish has completed four World Marathon Majors, Tokyo, Boston, Chicago, and New York. It is not a detail added to a biography to appear well-rounded. It is relevant to how he practises.

A surgeon who runs marathons understands recovery in a way that most do not. The weeks of disciplined preparation, the management of injury around a goal, the difference between pain that means stop and pain that means continue, these are not abstract concepts. They directly inform how he counsels patients on rehabilitation, return to sport, and what realistic recovery timelines look like.

It also reflects the same disposition that took him to Bologna, Birmingham, and Adelaide: an unwillingness to settle for good enough when better is available.

Philosophy

How Dr. Satish approaches every case

🎯

Subspecialty precision

Every case managed by a surgeon who trained specifically in that area, not a generalist covering multiple domains.

📖

Informed patients

A patient who understands their condition makes better decisions and recovers with better expectations. Explanation is part of the treatment.

🤝

Honest about uncertainty

Where outcomes vary, or where surgery is not the right answer, that will be said plainly, even when it is not what the patient hoped to hear.

"The best outcome for a patient is not always surgery. My job is to give you an accurate picture of your situation and an honest account of what surgery can and cannot achieve, then support whatever decision you make."
