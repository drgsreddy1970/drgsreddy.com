    Knee

    Knee conditions & treatments

    The knee is the most commonly affected joint in India, driven by lifestyle factors like floor-sitting and squatting, genetic predisposition, and an ageing population.

    🇮🇳
    Why knee arthritis is so common in India: Habitual squatting, sitting cross-legged, and climbing stairs in multi-storey homes place significantly higher stress on the knee joint than flat-surface walking. Indian patients often present with more advanced arthritis at the same age compared to Western populations, partly due to lifestyle, partly due to delayed consultation.

    Hip conditions & treatments

    Hip pain in India is often misattributed to the spine or "sciatica", delaying diagnosis. Hip arthritis, avascular necrosis, and fractures in the elderly are all common and very treatable.

    🇮🇳
    Avascular necrosis (AVN) of the hip is significantly more common in India than in Western countries. Overuse of steroids, including for skin conditions, allergies, and COVID treatment, is one of the most common causes seen in younger Indian patients.

    Bone Health

    Bone health & prevention

    Strong bones are the foundation of healthy joints and fracture-free ageing. Many Indian patients, particularly women and the elderly, have significantly lower bone density than they realise.

    🇮🇳
    Vitamin D deficiency is near-universal in urban India, paradoxically, despite abundant sunshine. Most urban Indians spend little time outdoors, and clothing coverage further limits sun exposure. Low Vitamin D directly impairs bone mineralisation, contributing to osteoporosis and fracture risk.

    Rehabilitation

    Recovery & rehabilitation

    Surgery is only half the story. What happens in the weeks and months after the operation determines how fully you recover. These guides cover physiotherapy, exercises, pain management, and getting back to daily life.

    🇮🇳
    Physiotherapy compliance is lower in India than it needs to be. Many patients discontinue exercises once pain reduces, well before strength and movement have fully returned. The patients who do best are those who follow through for the full recovery period, not just until they feel better.

    Why rehabilitation matters as much as the surgery

    A technically perfect operation with poor rehabilitation gives a poor result. A good operation with disciplined rehabilitation gives an excellent one. The implant or the repair creates the potential for recovery; the weeks of structured exercise afterwards are what convert that potential into a working joint. Strength, balance, range of motion, and confidence in the limb are all rebuilt in rehabilitation, not in the operating theatre.

    Every protocol below is organised into phases. Each phase has goals, precautions, exercises, and criteria that must be met before progressing to the next. Progression is based on what your body has achieved, not just on how many weeks have passed. Moving too fast risks damaging the surgery; moving too slow allows stiffness and weakness to set in.

    How to use these protocols

    1. Download your protocol Each protocol is available as a PDF. Download the one that matches your surgery.
    2. Share it with your physiotherapist These protocols are written to be used by your physiotherapist, wherever you are doing your rehabilitation.
    3. Progress by criteria, not dates The timelines are guides. Move to the next phase only when the progression criteria are met.
    4. When in doubt, ask Every recovery is individual. If something does not feel right, contact the clinic before pushing through.
